# Exploration

## Exploration

Rules for exploration are provided below, including overland, underground, water voyages and flight. Portable light sources are covered on p.53.

For a well provisioned party on a short trip, the GM might periodically check for random encounters, and call for an Int (Wilderness Lore) check. If failed, the explorers become temporarily lost, losing a day backtracking.

For longer, more substantial expeditions however, the following process is provided. Apply each day, 1d3 days, or other GM determined interval:

1. Roll on the *Weather* table to determine starting or changing weather, which may impact on choice of objective, random encounters, ease of navigation, etc,
2. PCs choose a specific site or area at large that they wish to reach or investigate,
3. PCs pick a route, often requiring choices between terrain (which will affect random encounters), travel speed, distances, and likely dangers.

1. Roll 1d20 for a *Travel Event* on the *Overland*, *Underground*, *Voyage* or *Flight* table. If an event occurs and the particular time is important, randomise day or night, then roll 1d12 for the hour.

Whilst travelling, the PCs might get up to a number of things including scouting, navigating, sneaking, foraging, talking to NPCs, and so on. At the GM's discretion, the party's travel speed (slow & careful, normal, fast & reckless, etc) might impose modifiers on such activities.

## Travel Speed

Simplified travel distances for an 8 hour trek are provided below (assuming daytime travel on foot and carrying a moderate to heavy load, ie weapons, armour, supplies and loot). Explorers can push themselves to travel twice as far by using every hour of daylight, but this leaves no time for foraging, is noisy, and may draw attention. A Con check is required at the end of a 16 hour forced march to maintain such a pace the next day.

Riders on horses or other beasts generally travel twice as quickly. Carrying extreme loads, bad weather, travelling at night, by stealth, and so on may reduce travel speed at the GM's discretion.

## Overland Events

Roll 1d20 to determine overland events whilst travelling. What follows are merely examples, GMs are encouraged to add/substitute their own custom entries.

### (1) Freak Weather

A freak weather change suddenly occurs. Roll for *Shifting Weather* (reroll 6 or 7), and exaggerate the effect (eg “less windy” might mean the air turns completely still and stale, making spoor hunting difficult, or “wetter” a sudden downpour washing away tracks). If the PCs press on through difficult weather, a Con (Athletics) check is required to avoid 1 Str loss due to exhaustion.

### (2) Off Course

The party has strayed from their intended course (if possible, otherwise ignore or reroll this result). A Party Challenge is required to regain bearings (focusing on Int, Perc, Wilderness Lore, Gather Information, General Lore).

If successful, the adventurers retrace their steps but lose a day’s travel. If unsuccessful, they become properly lost (GM secretly randomises their location on the map, and/or requires additional bearing checks).

### (3) Random Encounter

A *Random Encounter* occurs.

### (4) Vagaries of Adventure

The party experiences an unexpected turn of weal or woe (even chance). If good (the stars align, blessed from afar, lucky charm/shibboleth, dumb luck, etc) 1d4 PCs may reroll a single roll during the adventure at a time of their choosing (separate to their *Reroll Pool*).

If bad (wrong place wrong time, supernatural curse, ill fated prophesy, blind misfortune, lost mascot, etc) roll 1d6:

1. 1. 1d4 PCs are exposed to a random disease.

1. 2. 1d4 hirelings, henchmen or pets are exposed to a random disease.
2. 3. 1d4 PCs lose, damage (not usable until repaired), or use up a random item or batch of related items.
3. 4. The PCs have secretly been (or will be) betrayed by a hireling or other NPC, to be revealed at an opportune moment. If irrelevant to the adventure at hand, ignore this result.
4. 5. One PC misplaces, loses or has (secretly) stolen from them a valuable of some kind (coin pouch, gemstone, piece of jewellery, etc). A *Luck* (Perc) check resists.
5. 6. A *Random Encounter* occurs.

### (5) Ancient Ruins

Forgotten ruins beckon, tempting the adventurers with the possibility of treasure. Exploring the ancient site requires a Party Challenge (focusing on Int, Perc, Detection, Wilderness Lore, General Lore). If successful, each PC finds some loot (roll 1d6, 1-3: 1 x Carry Loot, 4-5: 1 x Trinkets & Curios, 6: 1 x Valuables). If unsuccessful, the party awakens the ruin’s guardian instead (*Boss Monster*; roll on an appropriate random encounter table).

### (6) Overwhelming Forces

Overwhelming numbers of enemies or other rivals block the party’s present course (xenophobic barbarians guard a bridge, enemy scouts fill the valley, a man eating skorn tribe is camped along the riverbank, etc). A Party Challenge focusing on Dex, Int, Perc, Stealth and Wilderness Lore is required to slip past. If unsuccessful, run a Chase to determine whether the adventurers escape.

### (7) Random Encounter

A *Random Encounter* occurs.

### (8) World Building

The explorers’ travel is relatively uneventful, providing ample time for study and contemplation. One player may propose a new piece of world building (place, history, NPC,

legend, etc). If most of the table is entertained, and GM agrees, the proposed material becomes part of the setting.

### (9) Natural Wonder

The party stumble upon a natural wonder, fey sacred site, or other place of special majesty or tranquillity. If the party spends a day here, one PC suffering madness may make a Will check to reduce the severity by one level.

### (10) Other Travellers

The party encounters other travellers, roll 1d8: (1) 1d3 scouts, (2-3) 2d6+10 borderguard, (4) merchant caravan, (5) 1d4+2 rival adventurers, (6) noble with entourage, (7) 4d6+10 religious pilgrims, (8) GM special.

Roll 2d6 to determine the travellers' initial reaction: (2-3) hostile, (4-5) suspicious, (6-8) neutral, (9-10) cautiously curious, (11-12) friendly. The travellers might have useful gear or information to trade, provide safety in numbers, draw unwanted attention, rob the PCs, threaten them as rivals, etc.

### (11) Random Encounter

A *Random Encounter* occurs.

### (12) Party Bonds

The explorers' travel is relatively uneventful, providing ample time to strengthen party bonds. One player may choose to reveal a secret or other special detail about their PC's history (a deed, place, NPC, event, party bond, prior adventure, etc - GM veto applies). If most of the table is entertained, one PC may transfer one point of *Luck* or a *Reroll* die to another PC (up to their normal maximum). Once per PC only.

### (13) Short Cut

The opportunity to take a short cut arises if the party can find it (unmarked river to sail, tunnels beneath the mountains, treetop rope bridges, game path through a bramble field, etc). Resolve as a Party Challenge focusing on Perc, Int,

Detection, Wilderness Lore and Gather Information. If successful, the party doubles its travel speed for one day.

### (14) Low Supplies

The party must make a Group *Luck* check or their rations (50%), water supply (30%) or both (20%) run out (tainted by parasites, spoiled by disease, stolen by animals, overeaten after too many ales, dropped in a chase, etc). Hunting for food and/or water requires a Party Challenge (focusing on Int, Perc, Wilderness Lore and Animal Lore).

A success gathers sufficient supplies for the remainder of the trip. A failure means the PCs go without (or subsist on quarter rations), causing 1 Con loss. Alternatively, the PCs may consume tainted food/water, avoiding Con loss, but requiring a Con check to avoid contracting a random disease.

### (15) Random Encounter

A *Random Encounter* occurs.

### (16) Inspiring Tale

The explorers' travel is relatively uneventful, providing ample time for stirring songs and grand tales. One player may choose to regale the group with a tune or story (historical, political, mythical, fable lesson, etc).

If most of the table is entertained, and the PC makes a Cha check, one random adventurer gains an immediate *Session Advance*. Once per PC only.

### (17) Unfortunate Injury

A random NPC (hireling, henchmen, other NPC) or animal (horse, mule, dog etc) falls ill, lame, or is otherwise injured (not including ranger pets). A Party Challenge focusing on Int, Cha, Will Apothecary and/or Animal Lore allows the injury to be managed before any lasting harm occurs. If unsuccessful, the NPC or animal is unable to continue under their own power.

### (18) Taxing Terrain

Difficult and unexpected terrain impedes the explorers' path (river crossing, forest fire, steep valley, landslide, ruined bridge, etc). Progress is slow and taxing, requiring a Party Challenge focusing on Str, Con, Will, Cha, Athletics, Leadership, Wilderness Lore. If failed, all PCs lose 1 point of Str, and a random PC uses up or loses a piece of relevant gear (rope, climbing gear, rations, torches, etc).

### (19) Random Encounter

*A Random Encounter occurs.*

### (20) Perfect Campsite

The party has a chance to locate the perfect campsite, providing comfort and safety in equal measure. A Perc (Wilderness Lore) check is required. On a success, all PCs restore 1 lost attribute point, 1 *Reroll* die, or 1 point of *Luck* (once per adventure only).

## Voyage Events

### (1) Weather Change

A substantial weather change occurs. Roll 1d6:

1. *Perfect weather*: Favourable winds, currents, clear passage and visibility; double the ship's travel speed for one day
2. *Ordinary Storm*: Moderate to heavy rain and high winds reduces visibility by a factor of 10. Ship travel speed is increased or decreased by 50% (even chance).
3. *Rolling Fog*: Visibility is reduced to 1d3 x 100 ft.
4. *Gale Force Winds*: Powerful winds double the ship's travel speed for one day, or break one of the masts, reducing speed by 50% until repaired at port (a *Luck* check determines which).
5. *Severe Storm*: Powerful winds, high waves and lashing rain reduce visibility to 1d4 x 100 ft, ship travel speed is halved, and there is a 50% chance the ship is wrecked on a reef, island, or is otherwise severely damaged (a group *Luck* check resists). At the GM's discretion, cargo might be damaged or crew lost overboard (*Luck* checks resist).
6. *Becalmed*: the ship covers no ground if its only means of propulsion is wind. A ship with oars travels at approximately 3 knots. A group *Luck* check is made at the end of each day to determine if the winds return.

### (2) Off Course

The ship has strayed from its intended course (if possible, otherwise ignore or reroll this result). A Party Challenge is required to regain bearings (focusing on Int, Perc, Sailing, Wilderness Lore, General Lore).

If successful, the ship steers back on course but loses one day's travel. If unsuccessful, the crew become properly lost (GM secretly randomises their location on the map, and/or requires additional bearing checks).

A Group *Luck* save garners some treasure (roll 1d6: 1-3: 1 x Valuables, 4-6: 1 x 3 HD Lair Treasure). There is a 50% chance a random number of PCs are exposed to disease.
